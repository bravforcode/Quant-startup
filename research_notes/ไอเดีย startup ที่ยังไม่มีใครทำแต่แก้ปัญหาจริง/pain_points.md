# Pain Points: Unmet Problems with Evidence of Cost (Thailand / SEA focus)

Research date: 2026-10-20. Source dates are given where the source shows one. Where a date was not shown in the search result, it is marked "date not shown". Most evidence below comes from WebSearch summaries and press coverage; I did not open most full documents, so figures should be re-checked against originals before use.

**Coverage limits (read first):** Reddit-specific search failed (WebSearch refused reddit.com as a domain), so Reddit evidence is limited to one GummySearch page. Pantip, Thai Facebook groups, Google Play / App Store reviews, G2 and Capterra complaint threads were not pulled. Global (non-SEA) candidates were not researched. NSO Thailand, World Bank and ILO SME statistics were not pulled directly. The 10-15 candidate target is met, but several candidates (marked "lead" or "weak") have thin evidence.

## Q1. Which problems repeat in complaints, forums and reviews with signs of willingness to pay?

### Takeaway
Direct forum evidence is the weakest part of this research. The strongest repeat-complaint signal found is about cash flow and late payment (small suppliers and SMEs waiting months to be paid), plus a small number of invoicing-tool support complaints. Most willingness-to-pay signal is indirect: SMEs paying interest on bridge loans, or paying for tools after billing problems.

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate 1: Late payment from large buyers to SME suppliers (Thailand).** Who: small suppliers selling to large modern-trade retailers and large buyers. Severity: some suppliers report waiting 6-8 months to be paid, per Trade Competition Commissioner Warawan Chitaroon; the stated causes are often routine paperwork and accounting checks. Coping: some retailers offered short-term loans during the wait, which adds interest cost for the supplier — https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40070834 (Sept 2026 per search summary) and https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/3319758/crackdown-on-large-companies-delaying-sme-payments (date not shown).
- **Candidate 1 (cont.): Spend.** Interest on bridge loans is cited as an added cost but no total is given — gap. Source: same as above.
- **Candidate 2: Invoicing and billing-tool support complaints (global, small share of evidence).** Trustpilot reviews of Invoice2Go from January 2026 describe a price increase, an account deletion with no explanation, and one reviewer losing access to sent invoices — https://www.trustpilot.com/review/invoice2go.com (Jan 2026 per search summary). A Figma forum post from June 2026 says invoicing issues had to be raised publicly to get support — https://forum.figma.com/report-a-problem-6/weird-to-have-to-publicly-discuss-invoicing-issues-to-get-support-54660 (June 2026 per search summary; this is a vendor forum, not a tool review).
- **Candidate 2 (cont.): Reddit signal.** GummySearch lists a popular r/invoicing post titled "Major Invoicing Problems Hampering Your Small Business Cashflow" — https://gummysearch.com/r/invoicing (date not shown; comments not visible in the search result). Only the title was seen.
- **Candidate 2 (cont.): Unverified claim.** A BigIdeasDB article says billing tools are plagued by poor customer support and cites Capterra and Reddit complaint data — https://bigideasdb.com/business-ideas/home-business (date not shown). I could not verify its method, so treat as unconfirmed.
- **Candidate 3: Leads not yet read (titles only).** These are headlines seen in search results, not verified contents:
  - "Urgent from CEO: send payment" — multimillion-dollar scams targeting Thai companies (business email compromise style) — https://www.thaipbsworld.com/crime/%E2%80%98urgent-from-ceo:-send-payment%E2%80%99-%E2%80%93-the-multimillion-dollar-scams-targeting-thai-companies (date not shown).
  - "'Buy now, pay later' abuse under scrutiny" — https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/2788502/buy-now-pay-later-abuse-under-scrutiny (date not shown).
  - "Workers protest over late wages" — https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2901043/workers-protest-over-late-wages (date not shown).

### Inferences
- Late payment is the most repeated Thai SME pain in what I found, and it is repeated in regulator and press coverage, not just forums. That makes it a candidate for a receivables, invoice-finance or collections product. Forum confirmation is still needed.
- Invoicing-tool complaints are about support and price changes, not about the core job of invoicing. That suggests a switching opportunity, but the evidence is 2-3 reviews plus one Reddit title. Weak.

### Gaps
- No Reddit comment threads, Pantip threads or Thai Facebook group posts were retrieved. The Reddit domain was blocked for WebSearch.
- No app-store or G2/Capterra complaint pull was done.
- I could not confirm willingness to pay for invoicing tools from any direct source.

## Q2. Which problems are created or amplified by 2025-2026 shifts (AI, labor, aging, regulation, cross-border commerce, SME digitization)?

### Takeaway
The clearest 2026 shifts are: a new Thai late-payment rule for SME credit terms (effective 2 November 2026 per one report), a credit squeeze on SMEs, slow move from cash to digital payments across SEA with SMEs struggling at checkout, and Thai e-invoicing that is voluntary for now but with unclear mandate timing.

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate 1 (regulation): New SME payment-term rules.** The Trade Competition Commission rules set 45-day maximum credit terms for SME trade, manufacturing and services, and 30 days for agricultural products. They apply to buyers with at least 500 million baht revenue. Breach is an unfair trade practice, with a maximum fine of 10% of revenue in the year of the offence — https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40071929 ("Thailand updates 30- and 45-day SME payment rules from Nov 2"; the later report says effective 2 November 2026). Note: an earlier article in the same search describes the penalty as a draft proposal (https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40070834). The later article is the more current account.
- **Candidate 4 (credit): SME loan rejection.** Bank of Thailand figures, cited by Governor Vitai Ratanakorn at a September 2026 forum (via press), put SME loan rejection at roughly 60-70%. Rejection is about 78% for SMEs with no credit history and about 86% for SMEs with a bad-debt record. Even among the top 30% of SMEs by performance, only 21% had credit access versus 61% of large firms in the same group. SME interest cost averages about 6.9% versus about 3% for large firms. SME loans contracted for 16 consecutive quarters — https://www.nationthailand.com/business/banking-finance/40070829 (Bank of Thailand sets out three steps to address SME credit crisis; Sept 2026 per search summary) and https://www.pattayamail.com/news/bank-of-thailand-plans-200-billion-baht-credit-push-as-sme-loan-rejections-soar-564470 (date not shown). The BOT plans a Credit Portal by end-2026 and a credit-guarantee mechanism targeted for 2027, using alternative data for SMEs with no traditional financial history. I did not find the original BOT publication; the numbers come from press reports.
- **Candidate 4 (cont.): Sales and debt pressure.** A TMBThanachart survey (figures per search summary, not verified in article) reports sales declines of about 4-5% at small businesses, with customers delaying payments and more SMEs delaying debt repayments to trade creditors — https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40070669 (headline "Thai SMEs face falling sales, bad debt and tight credit, await state aid"; date not shown). A separate article reports "debt tsunami" from fresh defaults and overdue loans — https://www.nationthailand.com/business/banking-finance/40070729 (date not shown; not read).
- **Candidate 5 (cross-border payments and SEA SME checkout).** An IDC study commissioned by 2C2P and Antom ("How Southeast Asia Buys and Pays 2026: Unlocking SMEs' Potential") surveyed 600 SMEs in six markets. Findings as reported in press: 66% of SMEs sell online; 49% engage in cross-border trade; three quarters plan to expand internationally within two years. Barriers cited: continued cash reliance, integration complexity, fraud concerns, high fees, infrastructure limits, transaction errors, data-integration issues and slow settlement. IDC estimates broader SME cross-border participation could add US$20.8 billion in regional sales by 2029 — https://e27.co/seas-smes-are-global-in-ambition-but-stuck-at-checkout-20260519/ (19 May 2026 per URL) and https://2c2p.com/news/sea-ecommerce-market-2029/ (date not shown). The study is vendor-commissioned.
- **Candidate 5 (cont.): Cash on delivery shift.** A Xendit/Cube report (2026, vendor) forecasts cash-on-delivery falling below 25% of regional e-commerce value in 2027. Cube estimates SEA shoppers spend roughly US$23-24 billion a year with overseas sellers; Singapore cross-border share is 19% and Indonesia is 6% — https://e27.co/cash-on-delivery-is-dying-in-southeast-asia-six-different-wallets-are-taking-its-place-20261015/ (15 Oct 2026 per URL). Note the IDC and Xendit/Cube regional e-commerce figures differ (US$289.8bn vs roughly US$400bn by 2031, different baselines); do not mix them.
- **Candidate 6 (regulation): Thai e-invoicing for SMEs.** Thai e-invoicing is voluntary for 2026; one July 2026 review says no B2B mandate is legislated for 2026-2027, and taxpayers transmit invoice data (XML) to the Revenue Department by the 15th of the following month. SMEs with turnover of THB 30 million or less can use a simplified email route (PDF/A-3 with an ETDA trusted time-stamp). The full route needs a certificate-based digital signature. The 200% tax deduction and 1% e-withholding rate are extended to end-2027, with implementing regulations pending — https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/5729-thailands-e-invoicing-remains-voluntary-key-20262027-updates-and-tax-incentives and https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/07/09/thailand-e-invoicing-e-reporting-country-booklet/ (July 2026 per URL). One vendor guide (https://vatit.com/country-guide/thailand/, date not shown) says a mandate is targeted for 2028; this conflicts with the others and I could not confirm it from an official source.
- **Candidate 7 (AI and SME workflows).** Not researched beyond search results; no evidence collected.

### Inferences
- The SME credit gap and the new 45-day payment rule together create a working-capital problem for Thai suppliers. Products that help suppliers get paid quickly, or that give lenders alternative cash-flow data, address the same underlying gap from two sides. The BOT's planned alternative-data approach suggests the credit side is opening up.
- Cross-border and COD shifts are real, but the evidence here is mostly vendor-published forecasts. Treat as directional.
- Thai e-invoicing is a compliance-timing problem. Uncertainty about mandate timing reduces urgency for SMEs, so willingness to pay for compliance software is unproven.

### Gaps
- No evidence collected on AI adoption pain among Thai SMEs.
- Original BOT and Trade Competition Commission documents were not read; figures rely on press.
- No Thai SME count or share-of-GDP figure was retrieved from NSO or the SME Office. This is needed to size candidate 4 and candidate 1.
- Mandate timing for Thai e-invoicing is contested; an official Revenue Department statement was not found.

## Q3. Where do job postings, government statistics or industry surveys show persistent gaps (skills, service capacity, compliance, payment friction, information asymmetry)?

### Takeaway
The clearest persistent gap is in care services: an ILO study projects a large shortfall of paid home-based care workers in Thailand to 2037, with high turnover and low wages already visible. There is also a skills and hiring gap (Robert Walters) and a debt-and-savings gap among low-income workers (UTCC survey).

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate 8: Care-worker shortage (Thailand).** An ILO study (October 2025) estimates the need for paid home-based care workers will rise by at least 70%, with up to 250,000 additional paid care workers needed, including about 55,000 migrants. Currently, half of paid home-care workers are domestic workers; the other half are nurses, nurse assistants, community care workers and others. The ILO notes many families cannot afford private care, and warns large gaps will persist without public investment, especially for low-income households — https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/thailand-faces-shortfall-250000-care-workers-2037-ilo-report-finds (Oct 2025 per search summary). Who: families with older members; care workers. Coping: families and domestic workers.
- **Candidate 8 (cont.): Turnover and training.** A 2025 Mahidol University thesis links caregiver turnover to low wages, high stress and lack of recognition — https://archive.cm.mahidol.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/5959/1/TP%20GM.002%202025.pdf (2025). A World Bank report (June 2021) found shortages of suitably trained aged-care workers and inadequate training for family carers — https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/thailand/publication/caring-for-thailand-s-aging-population (21 June 2021). A Thai health-system study found high caregiver turnover in institutional care and projected headcount roughly matching need in 2026 but shortages of nurses, social workers and care managers; this is an older projection — https://he04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/j_hsr/article/view/3519 (date not shown).
- **Candidate 9: Skilled hiring and low-income debt (Thailand).** Robert Walters Thailand reports over 67% of organizations cite lack of skilled and experienced candidates as their main hiring obstacle, and 97% plan salary increases (2026 per search summary; no URL captured). A University of the Thai Chamber of Commerce survey (April 2026) of 1,250 low-income workers reports 98% in debt, 79.1% with no savings (no URL captured). Separately, a GTAI report (German) says June 2026 labour-force data show 5.9 million workers had not completed primary school and 9.8 million had only primary schooling — https://www.gtai.de/de/trade/thailand/wirtschaftsumfeld/mangel-an-fachleuten-und-bald-auch-an-hilfskraeften-227988 (June 2026 data per search summary).
- **Candidate 10: Migrant and refugee labour.** Private sector is pressing the government to extend migrant worker permits to ease shortages (per search summary; source article not captured). A 2025 government move to expand refugee work rights was linked to labour shortages, with research results due in 2027 (source not captured). The widely cited IOM projection of more than five million worker shortfall appears to be from before 2026 and is not used here.
- **Caution on older figures:** A European ASEAN Business Centre finding (67% cite skilled-worker availability as the main challenge) appears to predate 2026; no date was shown, so not used as current.

### Inferences
- The care gap is the best-evidenced persistent gap found: it has an official forecast (ILO), a quality/turnover mechanism (thesis, World Bank) and a payment constraint (families cannot afford private care). The payment constraint matters for any business model: demand exists but ability to pay is limited.
- Hiring gap and worker-debt figures both suggest low-wage and skilled-worker finance and upskilling demand, but the sources are a recruiter and one survey without a public URL. Treat as directional.

### Gaps
- No Thai job-posting data (e.g., JobsDB, Jobthai) was pulled to measure vacancy duration or unfilled roles.
- NSO Thailand labour survey tables were not pulled directly; the GTAI figures are secondary.
- Robert Walters and UTCC sources need URL verification before citing.

## Q4. Which problems are painful for a large number of people or businesses, not just a niche?

### Takeaway
Breadth is strongest for Thai SMEs as a group (credit access and late payment affect many firms) and for households facing aging-related care needs. Breadth for invoicing tools, scams and BNPL is unproven; those are single-source leads.

### Cited Findings
- **Candidate 1 + 4 (SME working capital): broad.** Late payment, credit rejection and falling sales are reported across SMEs in general, not a niche — sources as above (Nation Thailand, Bangkok Post, Pattaya Mail). Specific counts of affected SMEs were not found.
- **Candidate 5 (SEA SME online and cross-border): broad but vendor-sourced.** IDC/2C2P: 600 SMEs surveyed across six markets; 66% sell online — https://e27.co/seas-smes-are-global-in-ambition-but-stuck-at-checkout-20260519/.
- **Candidate 8 (care): broad.** ILO projects demand growth of at least 70% for home-based paid care — https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/thailand-faces-shortfall-250000-care-workers-2037-ilo-report-finds.
- **Candidate 9 (worker debt): broad among low-income workers.** 98% in debt, per UTCC survey (no URL captured).

### Inferences
- The broadest, best-sourced pains are working-capital and payment timing for Thai SMEs, and unmet care capacity for older Thais. Both are large-population and recurring.
- A product must work within the limits of the affordability evidence: the ILO notes low-income families cannot pay for private care.

### Gaps
- No count of Thai SMEs, older adults or care workers was pulled from NSO or the SME Office.
- No willingness-to-pay data (price points, survey on spend) was found for any candidate except interest cost on SME bridge loans (6.9% average, BOT figures).
- Scams, BNPL abuse and late-wage protests are single-headline leads; breadth cannot be judged.
