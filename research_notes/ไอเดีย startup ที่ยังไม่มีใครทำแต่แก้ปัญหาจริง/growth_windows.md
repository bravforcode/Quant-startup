# Growth Windows 2026-2032: Global and Southeast Asia / Thailand

Research date: 2026-10-20. Scope: market windows with high growth potential for a new startup, 2026-2032.

Evidence tags used throughout:
- [FACT] official statistic or enacted/published regulation
- [EST] analyst or vendor forecast (vendor named where applicable)
- [OPN] my reasoning or opinion, not a sourced number

Method caveat: most figures below come from search-result excerpts. Primary pages were not fetched in full unless stated. Items marked UNVERIFIED need checking against the primary source before any use in a pitch or model.

## Which markets are inflecting from AI capability, agents, or cost curves (2025-2026)?

### Takeaway
Agent-based enterprise software has the largest published growth rates, but those figures come from vendor research and should be discounted. The underlying cost curve (inference price per unit of capability) is the most credible driver. Gartner's own data also shows high cancellation risk for agentic projects.

### Cited Findings
- [EST, vendor] AI agents market: MarketsandMarkets (Apr 2025) puts it at USD 7.84B in 2025, rising to USD 52.62B by 2030, a 46.3% CAGR (2025-2030). Base year 2024 is USD 5.26B. — [MarketsandMarkets via PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/ai-agents-market-worth-52-62-billion-by-2030---exclusive-report-by-marketsandmarkets-302435498.html); report page: [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/ai-agent-market-15761548.html)
- [EST, vendor] BCC Research: 2024 base USD 5.7B, USD 48.3B by 2030, 43.3% CAGR (2025-2030). — [BCC Research press release](https://www.bccresearch.com/pressroom/ait/ai-agents-market-to-grow-433-annually)
- [EST, vendor] Grand View Research (per secondary summary, not opened directly): USD 5.40B (2024) to USD 50.31B (2030), 45.8% growth rate. — [Techjury summary](https://techjury.net/industry-analysis/ai-agent-market-size-share/) (secondary; UNVERIFIED against primary)
- [EST, vendor] Precedence Research (per same Techjury summary): USD 7.92B (2025) to USD 236.03B (2034). Longer horizon, more aggressive; definitional spread noted by the summary. — [Techjury summary](https://techjury.net/industry-analysis/ai-agent-market-size-share/) (UNVERIFIED)
- [EST, analyst] Gartner (June 2025): 33% of enterprise software applications will include agentic AI by 2028, up from under 1% in 2024. Secondary reporting only; not verified on Gartner's page. — [SDxCentral summary](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/according-to-gartner-the-future-isnt-actually-agentic/) (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [EST, analyst] Gartner (25 June 2025 press release): autonomous agents to make at least 15% of day-to-day work decisions by 2028, from 0% in 2024. Secondary reporting; UNVERIFIED primary.
- [EST, analyst] Gartner press release (25 June 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027, due to escalating costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)
- [FACT, reported as Stanford AI Index 2025 with Epoch AI and Artificial Analysis data] Price of GPT-3.5-level MMLU performance fell from USD 20 to USD 0.07 per million tokens between Nov 2022 and Oct 2024 (more than 280x). Primary AI Index page not opened; figure taken from secondary coverage. — [PremAI summary](https://www.premai.io/blog/llm-token-cost/) (secondary; check primary Stanford AI Index 2025)
- [EST, Epoch AI, secondary] Epoch AI median price decline across benchmarks about 50x per year, about 200x per year since Jan 2024. — secondary summary, same source family (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [FACT-reported, single secondary] Total inference spend is rising while unit prices fall: a report (The Information, via secondary) puts OpenAI's 2025 inference bill near USD 8.4B, about 4x the prior year. UNVERIFIED. — [Sesame Disk summary](https://sesamedisk.com/ai-inference-cost-trends-2026-3/)
- [FACT] Gartner, press release: the cancellation forecast above is the only directly verified primary-source figure in this group.

### Inferences
- [OPN] The unit-cost decline is the structural driver. It makes agent workflows economically viable for SME-scale buyers in 2026-2032, not only for large enterprises. Price declines also compress margins for any startup whose value is "AI wrapper" only.
- [OPN] The gap between rising spend and falling unit prices suggests demand is growing faster than price declines, but I found no independent, verified market-level total spend figure.
- [OPN] Gartner's 40% cancellation forecast plus vendor CAGRs of 43-46% together imply most of the market value will come from a minority of agent deployments that show clear ROI. A startup should build around a measurable task outcome, not a generic "agent" product.

### Gaps
- No verified primary source for the 33% and 15% Gartner figures. Need Gartner's own report page.
- Vendor CAGRs (MarketsandMarkets, BCC, Grand View, Precedence) disagree widely on the long horizon and are not on comparable definitions. Techjury attributes the spread to definitions of "agent" versus traditional automation.
- No verified Thailand or SEA agent market size.
- Stanford AI Index 2026 was not found; the cited figures are from the 2025 edition.

### Ranking note
Strongest growth numbers in this set, weakest credibility of the exact figures. Adoption stage: **inflection** (enterprise adoption at early scale; Gartner's cancellation rate indicates a shakeout is ahead).

## Which growth is driven by demographics or labor shortages (aging, skills gaps, care work, SME labor)?

### Takeaway
Demographics are the most verifiable driver in this review. Thailand's 65+ share and the global care-worker gap are both supported by official or UN-based data. Market sizes for care services are not verified for either the world or Thailand.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT, UN WPP 2024 via German federal agency bpb] Share of people aged 65+ rose from 5.5% (1975) to 10.0% (2023); UN projects 18.5% by 2060. Over 800M people aged 65+ in 2023; 1.85B projected by 2060. — [bpb](https://www.bpb.de/kurz-knapp/zahlen-und-fakten/globalisierung/52811/demografischer-wandel/)
- [FACT, UNFPA 2024, as quoted in a search excerpt] Global 65+ share 10.3% in 2024; projected 20.7% by 2074. UNFPA primary link not confirmed. (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [EST, UN-based, secondary] Global 65+ population projected at 1.6B by 2050 (over 16% of world population). Attributed to "recent UN forecasts" only; not confirmed against WPP 2024. — [Anadolu/PNA, 2024](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/elderly-population-worldwide-to-surpass-youth-by-2030/3346179) (UNVERIFIED)
- [FACT, ILO 2018 flagship report, as quoted in search excerpt] People needing care to reach 2.3B by 2030 (up from 2.1B in 2015). About 269M new jobs could be created if investment in education, health and social work were doubled by 2030. — [ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/ilo-calls-urgent-action-prevent-looming-global-care-crisis) (excerpt-sourced; primary report not opened)
- [EST, ILO 2022 follow-up, excerpt] Meeting care needs could create almost 300M jobs by 2035. (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [FACT, ILO Future of Work podcast, undated excerpt] About 380M people work in care sectors worldwide, two-thirds women; low wages and poor conditions deter new entrants. Date not shown.
- [FACT, Thailand, UN WPP 2024 via World Bank/UN data (search excerpt)] People aged 65+ at 15.36% of population in 2024 (up from 14.72% in 2023). — [Statbase, World Bank/UN data](https://statbase.org/data/tha-population-age-65-and-above/)
- [FACT/EST, Thailand] Population aged 65+ in mid-2025 about 11.47M (UN WPP 2024 projection). Thai government gazette (IPSR, 2024) lists about 9.37M older persons at midyear 2024, so the two sources differ by roughly 2M. The official NSO 2025 figure was not found. — [IPSR Gazette 2025](https://ipsr.mahidol.ac.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Gazette2025EN.pdf)
- [EST, Thailand, NESDC via Statista] Population aged 60+ was 20.17% in 2023; forecast 31.37% in 2040. — [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/713667/thailand-forecast-aging-population)
- [FACT, Thailand, DBD registration data as reported] 2,331 registered senior-care businesses as of 30 June 2025: 1,475 home care, 810 long-stay facilities, 46 day-care centers; 98% are small enterprises. Long-stay facilities grew about 10% a year and home care about 6.8% a year (2022-2024), measured as business registrations, not revenue. — [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/thailands-senior-care-industry-emerges-as-high-growth-opportunity-528095) (secondary; check DBD)
- [FACT/EST, Thailand] Over 13M Thais aged 60+ (more than 20% of population). Number of Thais over 80 needing assistance projected to rise more than six-fold to about 2.5M over two decades. — same Pattaya Mail source (secondary)
- [EST, ILO projection, Nov 2025 excerpt] Thailand needs up to 250,000 additional paid care workers for long-term care. — [News Life and Health, 10 Nov 2025](https://www.asiainsurancereview.com/News/View-NewsLetter-Article/id/93486/Type/eDaily) (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [FACT, World Bank 2021] Thailand's Universal Coverage Scheme does not cover any institutional long-term care because of its cost. Government support largely limited to Thai citizens. — [World Bank, Labor Markets and Social Policy (2021)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/249641622725700707/pdf/Labor-Markets-and-Social-Policy-in-a-Rapidly-Transforming-Caring-for-Thailand-s-Aging-Population.pdf)
- [FACT, pricing, secondary 2025] Nursing home costs approximately THB 20,000-100,000+ per month depending on care level and location. — [ExpatFocus guide] (secondary, UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- [OPN] The demand is structural: 65+ population growth is largely locked in by demographics through 2032. The Thai care market is supply-constrained (workforce, institutional coverage), which favors software that increases caregiver productivity or formalizes home care.
- [OPN] Thailand's care sector is fragmented: 98% small operators, many without scale. This is a classic under-digitized profile: booking, staffing, billing, and compliance are likely informal or manual, but I did not find a source that measures this directly.
- [OPN] The gap between demand (rapid 65+ growth) and the UHC exclusion of institutional LTC means out-of-pocket payment and private insurance will be important. This is a payment-structure opportunity, not just a care-service opportunity.

### Gaps
- No verified market-size (USD or THB) figure for Thai elderly care or long-term care services.
- No verified global care-services market size with CAGR from a credible source.
- Official NSO 2025 Thai population figure not found; the 9.37M vs 11.47M discrepancy is unresolved.
- ILO 2026 care-economy publication not found; 2018 and 2022 figures are older.
- Thai labor-supply data (migrant care workers, licensing rules) was not researched.

### Ranking note
Demographic evidence is the most verifiable in this review. Adoption stage: **mature demand, early in technology adoption** (use in Thailand: early to inflection for formal care software).

## Which 2025-2026 regulatory changes create new compliance demand, permitted business models, or opened markets?

### Takeaway
Two regulatory tracks are moving: sustainability disclosure (EU CSRD scope narrowed; Thailand SEC rules still in consultation) and AI regulation (EU AI Act high-risk deadlines deferred). Thailand's digital ID framework is expanding. Thailand open finance is announced but its timeline is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings

**EU sustainability disclosure (CSRD)**
- [FACT, EU Omnibus I] CSRD applies only to EU companies with more than 1,000 employees AND more than EUR 450M net turnover (dual test). This replaces the earlier two-of-three test (250 employees / EUR 40M turnover / EUR 20M assets). — [PwC Luxembourg](https://www.pwc.lu/en/sustainable-finance/eu-finalises-agreement-on-omnibus.html); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251222-eu-sustainability-omnibus-i-detailed-omnibus)
- [FACT, EU] Revised scope applies to financial years starting on or after 1 January 2027. Non-EU groups: reporting from FY2028 if group EU turnover above EUR 450M and an EU subsidiary or branch above EUR 200M. — [PwC Luxembourg](https://www.pwc.lu/en/sustainable-finance/eu-finalises-agreement-on-omnibus.html)
- [FACT, EU] CSDDD limited to companies above 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5B net turnover. — same PwC source
- [FACT, reported date] Council final approval reported as 24 February 2026 (one source). Some sources describe the "2025" date as the political agreement only. — secondary; UNVERIFIED against Official Journal.

**Thailand sustainability disclosure (SEC, ISSB-aligned)**
- [FACT, consultation] SEC Thailand proposes ISSB-aligned disclosure. Scope 1 and 2 first; Scope 3 delayed with transition relief. Draft requires limited assurance for Scope 1 and 2. — [Slaughter and May, ESG in APAC 2025](https://www.slaughterandmay.com/services/practices/environmental-social-and-governance/esg-in-apac-2025/thailand/); [IFRS jurisdiction snapshot](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/sustainability-jurisdictions/pdf-snapshots/thailand-ifrs-snapshot.pdf)
- [CONFLICTING] Start year for SET50 companies: IFRS snapshot says FY2026 disclosure (reported in 2027); Slaughter and May says 2026 (reporting 2027), with all SET companies in 2028. Secondary sources give FY2026 for SET50. Final rule text and effective dates not confirmed.
- [FACT, reported] SEC held a public hearing on proposed amendments aligning rules with ISSB. Page dated 5 August 2026 (date header; not fully verified). — [Thailand Business News](https://www.thailand-business-news.com/markets/247279-sec-holds-public-hearing-on-proposed-amendments-to-sustainability-disclosure-rules-to-align-with-issb-standards)
- [FACT, IFRS S2] All 15 Scope 3 categories must be considered; disclose those material. — [Forvis Mazars Thailand](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/accounting/ifrs-s2-priorities-for-ghg-reporting-in-thailand)

**EU AI Act**
- [FACT, EU AI Act] Prohibited practices and AI-literacy duties apply from 2 February 2025 (not changed by the Omnibus). — [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/news/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-deadline-delay) (secondary)
- [FACT, provisional agreement] Digital Omnibus on AI would move Annex III stand-alone high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and Annex I embedded-product obligations to 2 August 2028. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/); [Sidley, 22 June 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)
- [CONFLICTING] Formal adoption status: one source says Parliament voted 16 June 2026 with Council approval pending; another says Council final approval 29 June 2026 with OJ publication expected July 2026 (blog, less authoritative). Official Journal publication not confirmed. Today is 20 October 2026, so the 2 August 2026 baseline date has passed.
- [CONFLICTING] Article 50 transparency: Gibson Dunn says largely on original schedule; CASRAI describes a grace period to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market.
- [FACT, Omnibus] Adds a new Article 5 prohibition on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines/)

**Thailand digital ID**
- [FACT, ETDA, 2026] Digital ID services linked to 1,797 government e-services (tax payments, health eligibility, utilities) under Phase 2 of the framework (2025-2027). 28 digital ID service licences issued to 23 organisations. — [OpenGov Asia](https://opengovasia.com/thailand-expands-digital-id-infrastructure-linking-1797-government-e-services/)
- [FACT, ETDA, April 2026] Approximately 162.63M cumulative accounts across ThaID, NDID and other platforms. Note: cumulative accounts, not unique users; not comparable with the "30M people" figure below. — [Biometric Update, June 2026](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/thailand-advances-digital-id-2-0-with-expanded-digital-trust-framework)
- [FACT, BoT official, Money20/20 Asia, April 2026] NDID and ThaiID serving almost 30M people; PromptPay over 92M registered accounts. — [FinTech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/130435/money2020asia/top-trends-from-money20-20-asia-2026/)
- [FACT, DGA] DGA mandatory digital ID standard for government agencies; agencies may choose providers such as NDID. Use of DGA system optional. — [PwC Japan column] (Japanese source; UNVERIFIED primary)

**Thailand open finance**
- [FACT, Bangkok Post] BoT open banking data initiative scheduled to become operational in 2026. Data sharing requires explicit individual consent. Article date not shown. — [Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/business/general/2877327/central-bank-introduces-open-banking-data-initiative)
- [PRIOR PLAN, older report] Phase 1 deposit account data, then lending. Plan predates 2026 target and may have changed.
- [CONFLICTING/UNVERIFIED] Third-party tracker lists Thailand open banking as "in progress" with APIs under development. — [Fiskil open finance tracker](https://www.fiskil.com/open-finance-tracker/thailand)
- [FACT, June 2026] BoT preparing new guidelines for Buy Now Pay Later services. — no primary link found; secondary governor briefing reference (UNVERIFIED)

**Thailand e-invoicing**
- [FACT, July 2026, legal-status reports] No legislated B2B e-invoicing mandate for 2026 or 2027. The RD e-Tax Invoice & e-Receipt system is opt-in. Simplified e-Tax Invoice by Email route aimed at businesses with annual revenue up to THB 30M. — [Fiscal Requirements](https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/5729-thailands-e-invoicing-remains-voluntary-key-20262027-updates-and-tax-incentives); [Global Law Experts, reviewed 25 July 2026](https://globallawexperts.com/is-einvoicing-mandatory-in-thailand/)
- [FACT, incentive] 200% deduction for eligible e-Tax Invoice, e-Receipt and e-Withholding investments; extension to end-2027 approved June 2026, formal rules pending. — same Fiscal Requirements/Global Law Experts sources (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [CONFLICTING, unreliable] One secondary source says a mandate is targeted for 2028; a Thai vendor blog says e-Tax Invoice is still required for every SME. Both contradict the legal-status sources. Treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- [OPN] Sustainability disclosure creates compliance demand that cascades down supply chains: large firms need Scope 1 and 2 data from suppliers, including SMEs that lack systems. The narrowing of CSRD (from roughly 50,000 to a much smaller set; count not verified here) reduces the addressable buyer base in the EU, but Thailand's SET50 start still creates a concentrated buyer set.
- [OPN] The EU AI Act deferral lowers near-term urgency. Compliance tooling demand will still form, but the timing is shifted by about 16 months for Annex III and about 2 years for Annex I. Plan for a 2027 demand peak rather than 2026.
- [OPN] Thai digital ID is expanding fast with state backing. A private startup's niche is likely in verified onboarding, KYC for SME lending, and identity-linked workflows, not in running identity infrastructure itself. The state platform can absorb features.
- [OPN] Open finance is a regulatory unlock, but the timeline is uncertain and phased. Expect a 2027-2028 window for lending use cases if the 2026 data initiative goes live on schedule.

### Gaps
- EU CSRD: count of companies in scope under the final Omnibus not verified.
- Thailand SEC: final rule text, effective date, and assurance requirements not confirmed; SET50 start year conflicts across sources.
- EU AI Act: Official Journal publication and final Article 50 treatment not confirmed.
- Thailand BoT open finance: go-live date, final phasing, and consent framework not confirmed from primary source.
- Thailand DGA: no 2026 DGA-specific service count or NDID expansion announcement found.
- UK, Singapore MAS, and EU open banking/PSD3 regulation not researched in this pass.

### Ranking note
Regulatory windows are real but timing is uncertain. Adoption stage: CSRD (EU large cap) **inflection**; Thai SEC disclosure **early**; EU AI Act compliance **early** (deferred); Thai digital ID **inflection**; Thai open finance **early**.

## Which fast-growing markets are still dominated by manual or informal processes?

### Takeaway
Thai SME administration (invoicing, bookkeeping, payment reconciliation, tax reporting), Thai senior-care operations (home care and small facilities, 98% small), and care-worker recruitment and scheduling are the strongest candidates. Evidence of informality is indirect (business counts, the absence of mandates, and reported recruitment gaps), and I did not find a source that measures manual process share directly.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Thai e-Tax invoicing remains voluntary as of July 2026; no mandate legislated for 2026-2027. — [Fiscal Requirements](https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/5729-thailands-e-invoicing-remains-voluntary-key-20262027-updates-and-tax-incentives)
- [FACT, OSMEP/OECD] SMEs are 99.5% of enterprises and 68.8% of employment. — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/financing-smes-and-entrepreneurs-2026_bb7fc5c4-en/thailand_82ede51e-en.html)
- [FACT, DBD] 98% of 2,331 senior-care businesses are small enterprises. — [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/news/thailands-senior-care-industry-emerges-as-high-growth-opportunity-528095)
- [FACT, ILO projection] Up to 250,000 additional paid care workers needed in Thailand for long-term care. — [News Life and Health](https://www.asiainsurancereview.com/News/View-NewsLetter-Article/id/93486/Type/eDaily) (UNVERIFIED primary)
- [FACT, World Bank 2021] Shortage of trained aged-care workers. — [World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/249641622725700707/pdf/Labor-Markets-and-Social-Policy-in-a-Rapidly-Transforming-Caring-for-Thailand-s-Aging-Population.pdf)
- [FACT, Thailand digital ID] Digital ID covers 1,797 government e-services; PromptPay 92M+ registered accounts. Digital rails are already strong in consumer payments and government, which means the under-digitized step is more likely in B2B and SME workflows. — [OpenGov Asia](https://opengovasia.com/thailand-expands-digital-id-infrastructure-linking-1797-government-e-services/); [FinTech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/130435/money2020asia/top-trends-from-money20-20-asia-2026/)

### Inferences
- [OPN] Under-digitized B2B SME workflows in Thailand are a candidate window. The e-invoicing mandate is undecided, which means adoption is voluntary and the pull is weak until a mandate is set. The 200% deduction offers a near-term incentive. A startup can time a product to the mandate, not wait for it.
- [OPN] Senior care is under-digitized by structure: many small operators, workforce shortage, and an institutional-care gap in UHC. Software that cuts admin per caregiver is a plausible wedge, but unit economics depend on who pays (families, insurers, government).
- [OPN] Thailand's consumer payments are highly digital (PromptPay), so consumer-facing "digitize the payment" plays are likely crowded. Under-digitized value is more likely in SME back-office and in care-sector operations.

### Gaps
- No measured share of manual or paper-based SME invoicing in Thailand.
- No measured share of informal care work or unregistered care operators.
- Migrant care-worker recruitment and labor-market informality not researched.

## Ranked view of growth windows (synthesis)

### Takeaway

### Thailand view (summary)
- **Strongest demographic window:** aging and care. 65+ share 15.36% (2024); 60+ share 20.17% (2023), forecast 31.37% (2040) [EST]. Formal care sector is small and fragmented (2,331 operators, 98% small). ILO projects up to 250,000 additional care workers needed. Institutional LTC is not covered by UHC.
- **Strongest rails already in place:** digital ID (inflection) and PromptPay (92M registered accounts). The under-digitized step is B2B and SME workflow, not consumer payments.
- **Regulatory timing:** open finance (2026 target, unconfirmed); SEC sustainability disclosure (consultation, FY2026 or 2027 start for SET50, conflicting); e-invoicing (no mandate for 2026-2027; 200% deduction through end-2027 pending formal rules).
- **SEA context:** e-commerce GMV forecast over USD 300B (2025), growing about 15% YoY (forecast). Mature and platform-led; less attractive for a new entrant unless the wedge is a specific underserved segment.
