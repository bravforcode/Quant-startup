# Thai senior care (long-term care) market: research notes (as of 2026-10-20)

Scope: Thailand, with SEA comparators. Sources were found through web search. Most claims come from search snippets; a few pages were read in full (noted where relevant). No market figure below was invented. Where a source gives no method, that is stated.

Labels: FACT = a primary or official source states it (still a claim by that source). ESTIMATE = vendor, research-firm, or modelled figure. OPINION = analyst view. UNVERIFIED = found but not confirmable, internally inconsistent, or scope unclear.

---

## 0. Summary of what the sources support

- There is no single reliable market-size figure. Registry-based figures (DBD and SPC) put registered operators' revenue at roughly 1.3 to 2.0 bn THB (FACT, see 1.1). Vendor reports range from roughly 2.5 bn THB (2024, TTB) to USD 7.7 bn (2025, Grand View Research, all payers, all LTC). The gap is mostly scope, not just error.
- Operator counts depend on classification. DBD counts range from 776 to 2,331 across 2025 and 2026 releases. Licensed-facility and property-survey counts differ again (section 1.2 and 1.3).
- Public LTC money (NHSO, local governments) is large and mostly delivered as community care through local government and trained caregivers, not as market revenue for private operators.
- Caregiver supply is thin relative to stated need (ILO, Oct 2025). Pay for government-funded caregivers is low (5,000 to 6,000 THB per month).
- 2026 policy items are mostly proposals, budget lines, or campaign promises. None is shown to be enacted in these sources.

## 1. Market size (home care and institutional care)

### 1.1 Registry-based (DBD / SPC): the most traceable figures

- [FACT] 2,331 active juristic persons in "senior care business" at 30 Jun 2025. Home Care 1,475; Long Stay 810; Day Care 46. 98.63% are small companies. Registered capital 16,182.26 m THB (of which Thai 15,639.87 m; foreign 542.39 m, mostly in Home Care). Source: https://www.tpso.go.th/news/2512-0000000001 (SPC; undated page, data as of 30 Jun 2025). Also reported by Pattaya Mail, https://www.pattayamail.com/news/thailands-senior-care-industry-emerges-as-high-growth-opportunity-528095 (2025-12-02). Method: DBD juristic-person data, grouped into three types by SPC. The TSIC or activity codes used are not given.
- [FACT] Growth 2565 to 2568: Long Stay +9.97% a year, Home Care +6.79%, Day Care -3.3% (SPC, same source as above).
- [FACT] Location in 2567: Bangkok 928 (41.58%), Central excluding Bangkok 491 (22.0%), North 241 (10.8%). Same SPC source.
- [FACT] DBD, as of 7 Mar 2025: 776 active juristic persons; registered capital 4,236 m THB; 2566 revenue 1,295 m THB. Source: https://www.dbd.go.th/news/5911032568 (page undated; cites 7 Mar 2025).
- [FACT] DBD, Nov 2025: 844 juristic persons (609 in 2565); registered capital >4,400 m THB; revenue >2,000 m THB. Source: https://www.posttoday.com/smart-sme/734899 (2025-12-12).
- [FACT] DBD DataWarehouse+, as of 31 May 2026: 912 juristic persons (+13% y/y); registered capital 4,661 m THB; 2567 revenue >2,010 m THB. Source: https://www.topnews.co.th/news/1612277 (2026-06-25).
- [UNVERIFIED] The four DBD series (776, 844, 912, 2,331) do not reconcile. They likely use different activity-code scopes. The DBD code list was not found, so none of the counts can be converted into another. Revenue figures are from entities that filed statements in each year, so they are not full-market totals.
- [ESTIMATE] Ministry of Commerce (SPC) puts the senior-care business market at about 2,574 m THB in 2566 (2023), with 25.1% five-year growth. Methodology not disclosed. Source: https://suratthani.moc.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/112/iid/136333 (2025-12-02).

### 1.2 Licensed facilities (Department of Health Service Support, DHSS / ESTA)

- [FACT] A published list of licensed private senior-care establishments totals 927 (by type: Type 1 day care without overnight = 5; Type 2 residence = 17; Type 3 with overnight stay = 905). Source: https://www.dop.go.th/download/knowledge/th1743570381-2609_0.pdf (file metadata 2025-02-03; the document itself is undated).
- [UNVERIFIED] The same PDF also says "908" in a header, and gives Type 1 as 4 in one place and 5 in another. Count depends on the date of extraction.
- [FACT] The licence system has a public lookup: https://esta.hss.moph.go.th/check-shops.php (live list; no count visible in the snippet).
- [UNVERIFIED] Whether a home-care dispatch agency needs this licence, or only facility operators, was not confirmed.

### 1.3 Property and real-estate surveys

- [FACT] REIC survey via LH Bank industry outlook (Mar 2025): 916 senior-housing projects, of which 832 are nursing homes (90.8%) and 84 residences; 516 (63.2%) are in Bangkok metro. Source: https://www.lhbank.co.th/getattachment/524daa4a-51f1-4ad4-9729-1d364c00d796/economic-analysis-Industry-Outlook-2025-Residential_Care_Mar25 (date from file name; Mar 2025).
- [FACT] REIC (as reported 2025-03-31): 785 senior-care centres with total capacity 20,000 beds. Source: https://www.thailand-business-news.com/lifestyle/206029-thailand-tackles-aging-crisis-by-expanding-elderly-care-industry (2025-03-31). 785 and 832 are from the same body and differ, so methods differ.

### 1.4 Institutional care revenue (LH Bank analysis of DBD statements)

- [FACT] Residential-care businesses (TSIC 87301, companies that filed statements): 2566 revenue 1,099 m THB, +8.8% y/y, CAGR about 23.7% over five years. Source: LH Bank PDF above (Mar 2025).
- [FACT] SME Promotion Office count, 2566: 626 operators (496 micro = 79.2%; 130 SME; no large). Same PDF.
- [FACT] Per LH Bank's reading of the filings, net profit has been negative for a sustained period, due to high start-up capital, a niche mid-to-high-income market, and competition in Bangkok. Same PDF. This is an analyst reading, so treat it as OPINION.
- [FACT] Nursing-home rent: most fall in 15,001 to 20,000 THB/month (REIC survey, same PDF). Public residences mostly ≤10,000 THB; private residences often 30,001 to 50,000 THB.

### 1.5 Home-care segment

- [UNVERIFIED] No sourced THB revenue figure for the home-care segment alone was found. The only home-care counts are the DBD entity counts in 1.1 (1,475 in mid-2025).

### 1.6 Vendor and research-firm estimates (all ESTIMATE)

- [ESTIMATE] Grand View Research: Thailand long-term care market USD 7.7 bn (2025), 8.3 bn (2026), 12.5 bn (2033); CAGR 6.1% (2026 to 2033). Out-of-pocket 63.64% share in 2025. Public is fastest-growing payer. Source: https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/long-term-care-market/thailand (undated; base year 2025). Method not disclosed beyond "Horizon Databook". Scope covers all LTC services (home healthcare, assisted living, hospice, nursing care) and all payers.
- [OPINION] At an exchange rate of about 32 THB/USD (assumed, not sourced), USD 7.7 bn is roughly 250 bn THB. That is about 100 times DBD-reported revenue for registered senior-care entities (about 2 bn THB). The scope is too wide to use as a bottom-up figure for private operators.
- [ESTIMATE] TTB Analytics (via Thailand Business News, 2025-03-31): professional elderly-care sector valued at 2.5 bn THB in 2024 (DBD data); forecast 30% annual growth to 20 bn THB by 2033.
- [UNVERIFIED] Internal inconsistency in the TTB figure: 2.5 bn THB growing to 20 bn THB over 2024 to 2033 (9 years) implies about 26% CAGR, not 30%. Either the growth rate or the endpoint is wrong, or the base year differs.
- [UNVERIFIED] 6Wresearch "Thailand Elderly Care Market" and "Long-Term Care Services Market" reports (2024, 2025; updated 2025 and 2026): titles and tables of contents only. No figures retrieved. Paywalled.
- [UNVERIFIED] A Deloitte estimate via LH Bank of "330,000 bn USD" elderly consumption by 2030 is not plausible for Thailand's scale. The unit is probably wrong. Not used.

### 1.7 Public spending (not market revenue for private operators)

- [FACT] Elderly welfare budget FY2026: 365 bn THB, reported as up about 50% over ten years. Covers cash welfare and allowances, not care services. Source: https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/3105929/budget-for-elderly-welfare-ballooning (2025-09-17, citing Budget Bureau). Snippet text is partly garbled; the headline figure is legible.
- [FACT] NHSO LTC per-person payment raised from 6,000 to 10,442 THB per year (board approval, 2024). Dementia (moderate) and end-of-life added. Expected coverage about 600,000 people, up from 320,000. Source: https://radiosukhothai.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/2367/iid/300628 (2024-06-25; reprint of NHSO news).
- [FACT] NHSO plan (Jul 2025 briefing): 18,587 care assistants at 1,115 m THB, to serve 106,807 dependent people, funded from the UCS dependent-care budget. Sources: https://www.nhso.go.th/th/communicate-th/thnewsforperson/30-8 (2025-08-26); https://www.nhso.go.th/en/news-events/news-announcements/thailand-expands-long-term-care-workforce-to-support-elderly-and-boost-rural-incomes (July 2025 briefing; English page dated 2025-12-17).
- [UNVERIFIED] Opposition MP (Ekapop, People's Party), Budget Bill debate, 30 Jun 2026: FY2570 LTC budget about 7,200 m THB for about 500,000 patients; average home visit 28 minutes per week against a recommended 90 to 180. Also 7,225 volunteer nurses at 1,147 m THB. Source: https://theactive.thaipbs.or.th/news/public-health-20260630-3 (2026-06-30). Figures are as stated by the MP, not checked against the budget bill.

## 2. Who pays

### 2.1 Households (out-of-pocket)

- [FACT] Survey of Older Persons in Thailand (SOPT 2021), as cited by ILO: among dependent people aged 60+, 86% get care from an unpaid carer (usually family); 4% get care from a paid worker; 10% get no care though they need it. Source: https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/ILO%20Care%20at%20home%20-%20Projecting%20Thailand%27s%20need%20for%20national%20and%20migrant%20labour%20for%20home-based%20care%20for%20older%20persons%202025-10-21%20FINAL.pdf (ILO, 2025-10-21).
- [FACT] Same ILO source: median monthly household income for older people is 12,000 THB overall; 15,000 THB for ages 60 to 69 with low dependency; 8,800 THB for those over 80. The lowest bracket (0 to 29,999 THB/year) holds most of the oldest and most dependent.
- [FACT] "In situations where publicly funded care does not fully meet needs, access to paid care depends on the older person (or family) paying." Same ILO source.
- [ESTIMATE] Grand View: out-of-pocket is the largest payer (63.64% in 2025). See 1.6.

### 2.2 NHSO (สปสช.): LTC for dependent elderly (ระบบดูแลระยะยาว)

- [FACT] Eligibility: age 60+ with Barthel ADL ≤11, home-bound or bed-bound. Includes moderate dementia and palliative care. Source: https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39855 (DOH deputy director, 2026-10-10); https://radiosukhothai.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/2367/iid/300628 (2024-06-25).
- [FACT] Coverage extended beyond UCS: from FY2020, NHSO LTC covers people in the CSMBS and Social Security schemes who are home- or bed-bound. Source: https://media.nhso.go.th/assets/portals/1/files/63-1_LTC_Book%28Eng%29.pdf (NHSO LTC book, undated; refers to FY2020).
- [FACT] Delivery runs through sub-district administrative organisations (SAOs), which submit care plans; NHSO reimburses on a capped budget. Some services (home modifications, respite) come from local welfare funds. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111867/ (academic review, 2026-04-22).
- [FACT, as cited] Same review: in some provinces only 20 to 25% of allocated LTC funds are used, because of reimbursement procedures. Source cited there: ref 8. Not independently checked.
- [FACT] Older (2016) handbook packages: lump sum 3,000 to 10,000 THB per person per year by group (group 1: ≤4,000; group 4: 5,000 to 10,000). Superseded by the 10,442 THB average from 2024. Source: NHSO LTC book (above).

### 2.3 Social Security Office (สปส.)

- [FACT] Old-age benefit: pension needs 180 months of contributions and age 55; monthly pension is 20% of average wage over the last 60 months, +1.5% per 12 extra months beyond 180. Lump sum if below 180 months. Sources: https://www.sso.go.th/wpr/main/privilege/... (SSO page, undated); https://lb.mol.go.th/en/old-age (Ministry of Labour e-Labour, undated).
- [UNVERIFIED] No SSO long-term-care or home-care benefit was found in SSO pages retrieved. This is an absence in search results, not proof that none exists.
- [FACT] SSO-insured people can access care through NHSO LTC (see 2.2), not through an SSO care benefit.

### 2.4 Elderly old-age allowance (เบี้ยยังชีพ), Ministry of Interior

- [FACT] Stepped monthly allowance: 60 to 69 = 600 THB; 70 to 79 = 700; 80 to 89 = 800; 90+ = 1,000. Under Ministry of Interior regulation effective 12 Aug 2023 (B.E. 2566). Sources: https://www.dop.go.th/th/faq/view=967 (undated); https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/31/iid/484396 (page refers to 2569); https://angthong.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/9/iid/206892 (regulation summary).
- [FACT] Government denied rumours of a 3,000 THB allowance (PM's office spokesperson, 2026-05-03: https://www.nationtv.tv/news/social/378976911) and a 1,250 THB allowance from Oct 2025 (DOP fact-check, 2025-07-08: https://www.dop.go.th/th/news/1/5769). Amounts remain 600 to 1,000.

### 2.5 Employers and insurers (private)

- [FACT] Health at Home sells corporate telemedicine ("Health at Work") to 500+ companies and partners with nine insurers and 100+ pharmacies (company-stated). Source: https://www.thestorythailand.com/health-at-home/ (2025-04-01).
- [UNVERIFIED] VNurse Care says insurers Bangkok Life, Ocean Life, True and Noble Development are customers or partners. Company press release only: https://thailandtv.news/vnurse-care-unveils-thailands-first-native-healthcare-app/ (2025-11-24).

## 3. Policy and 2026 changes

- [FACT] 2026-01-22: National Health Commission Office (สช.) gave five political parties a "one village, one caregiver" proposal: 100,000 community carers at 9,000 THB/month, phased over 5 to 10 years; about 10,000 m THB/year; a separate national LTC fund with local-government and private co-investment. Status: proposal (no enactment shown). Source: https://www.nationalhealth.or.th/NHCO/240 (2026-01-23).
- [UNVERIFIED] The proposal's arithmetic: 100,000 × 9,000 × 12 = 10,800 m THB/year. The source gives 9,000 m THB for wages and 1,000 m for monitoring at about 10 bn total. The 9,000 m figure implies 10 months, not 12. Flag and recheck before quoting.
- [FACT] NHSO Jan 2026 statement (in the same source): about 18,000 government-funded care assistants employed since 2016 (trained under a 70-hour Department of Health course); about 340,000 dependent elderly people (สช. figure).
- [FACT] DOH plan for FY2570 (Oct 2026): expand LTC through the "3C PLUS" online platform linked to NHSO; upgrade caregivers to "Super CG" with rehab skills; day care near home. Source: https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39855 (2026-10-10).
- [FACT] FY2570 budget (debate, Jun 2026): 7,225 volunteer nurses at 1,147 m THB, reported as cut from the earlier plan (in duration and posts). Source: The Active (above). Final passage status UNVERIFIED.
- [FACT] DBD and SHSTA (Thai senior healthcare association) 2026 excellence awards: 56 awards, to set quality standards for operators. Source: https://www.topnews.co.th/news/1612277 (2026-06-25).
- [FACT] SPC (Ministry of Commerce) recommends: service-quality standards; financing and insurance access; anti-nominee enforcement against foreign-owned operators using Thai names; a senior-care business database; training; tech adoption. Source: https://www.tpso.go.th/news/2512-0000000001 (SPC; undated; reported 2025-12-02).
- [FACT] Ministerial Regulation No. 15 (2024) on domestic workers caps working hours at 8 per day; ILO says it applies to paid home-care workers. Source: ILO (above).
- [OPINION] PMC review (2026): Thailand has no dedicated LTC insurance; a contributory layer "may offer a more sustainable approach" but is "politically sensitive". Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111867/ (2026-04-22).

### 4.3 Caregiver wages

- [FACT] NHSO-funded community care assistant: 5,000 THB/month (minimum 70-hour training) or 6,000 THB/month (120 hours). One-year contract. Minimum 8 hours/day and 20 days/month. Source: https://www.nhso.go.th/en/news-events/news-announcements/thailand-expands-long-term-care-workforce-to-support-elderly-and-boost-rural-incomes (July 2025); https://www.nhso.go.th/th/communicate-th/thnewsforperson/30-7 (2025-08-19).
- [FACT] Trained (70-hour) caregivers in Health Region 4 (n=232; mean age 56; 93.1% female; monthly income mostly 5,000 to 15,000 THB). Source: https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JCCPH/article/view/280437 (Journal of Council of Community Public Health, 2025-08-30). Snippet describes income bands; exact share not extracted.
- [FACT] Nursing-home staff pay (operator rate card): caregiver 13,000–18,000; assistant nurse 16,000–20,000 per month. Source: https://www.suneenursinghome.com/rate (undated).
- [FACT] ILO: care work is "low status and low paid, involving hard work and poor labour conditions" (author statement). Source: ILO (above, 2025-10-21).
- [UNVERIFIED] Private 24h caregiver take-home 14,000–25,000 (aggregator). Method not given.
- [FACT] Health-policy proposal: 9,000 THB/month for community carers (2026-01, not enacted; see section 3).

### 4.4 Caregiver shortage: evidence

- [ESTIMATE] ILO projection (Oct 2025): demand for paid home-based care to rise at least 70% by 2037. Upper scenario: up to 250,000 additional paid workers (over 13 times the current number), about 55,000 migrant. Reduced to under 100,000 with community and tech care. Source: https://thailand.un.org/en/309629-thailand-faces-shortfall-250000-care-workers-2037-ilo-report-finds (UN Thailand, 2025-10-29). ILO report: https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-10/... (2025-10-21). These are projections, so they are ESTIMATE.
- [FACT, derived] "Over 13 times the current number" implies a current paid home-based base of under about 19,000. The ILO base figure was not seen, so this is a derived bound.
- [FACT] Half of all paid care workers are domestic workers; the paid care workforce is increasingly migrant (ILO, 2025-10-21).
- [FACT] NHSO (Jan 2026): about 18,000 caregivers employed since 2016 against about 340,000 dependent elderly (สช. figure). Source: https://www.nationalhealth.or.th/NHCO/240 (2026-01-23).
- [UNVERIFIED] Denominators conflict. Dependent elderly: 340,000 (สช., Jan 2026); 500,000 (MP, FY2570, Jun 2026); 560,461 in community (DOH, Oct 2026); about 600,000 expected (NHSO, 2024). Caregivers: 135,591 (DOH, Oct 2026; scope unclear, possibly including trained volunteers) versus 18,587 NHSO-funded assistants.
- [FACT] Caregiver volunteers under the scheme are paid low, have no career path, and have no clear quality standard (MP, Jun 2026; UNVERIFIED as stated by MP).

## 6. Data conflicts and gaps (summary)

1. Operator counts: DBD 776 (Mar 2025), 844 (Nov 2025), 912 (May 2026), 2,331 (Jun 2025); licensed facilities 927 or 908 (undated); REIC 785 centres and 832 nursing-home projects. Scope and code list not reconciled.

3. Dependent-elderly population: 340k, 500k, 560k, 600k across sources.
4. Caregiver counts: 135,591 (DOH) versus 18,587 NHSO-funded assistants; scope unclear.
5. Proposal arithmetic: 9,000 THB × 100,000 carers × 12 months = 10,800 m THB/year; the source gives 9,000 m for wages.
6. SSO long-term-care benefit: no source found for or against.
7. Funding and traction for most Thai platforms are not public. Health at Home's funding amounts are not reliable (garbled Tracxn figures).
