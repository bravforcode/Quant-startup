# Thai credit-term rule: large buyers paying SME suppliers

Research notes. Checked on 2026-10-20 (today's date in session).

Labels: **FACT** (official text or primary document, read by us), **ESTIMATE**, **OPINION**, **UNVERIFIED** (could not confirm from a primary source).

Thai official names are kept in Thai. Dates are given as published (CE, with Buddhist Era in brackets where the source gives it).

## 0. Bottom line

- The rule is a **final, gazetted notification**, not a draft. It was published in ราชกิจจานุเบกษา on **2 ตุลาคม 2569 (2 Oct 2026)**, Vol. 143, ตอนพิเศษ 239 ง, pp. 43–46. Effective date: **2 พฤศจิกายน 2569 (2 Nov 2026)**.
- It is an ประกาศคณะกรรมการการแข่งขันทางการค้า (TCCT notification) under ss. 17(2) and 57(4) of the Trade Competition Act B.E. 2560 (2017).
- Caveat: I could read the gazette **header** but not the gazette **body** (text layer is garbled in our fetch). The clause text below comes from the TCCT-hosted PDF, which is watermarked **"ร่าง" (draft)**, plus secondary summaries. Word-for-word identity with the gazetted text is **UNVERIFIED**, though the figures match across every source.
- Core limits (FACT, from clauses 5 and 9): 45 days for trade, manufacturing and services; 30 days for agricultural goods and primary-processed agricultural goods with uncomplicated processing; fines up to 10% of revenue in the year of the offence (first-year businesses: up to THB 1m).
- The 45/30-day limit is not a hard cap. A different term is allowed with "reasonable business, marketing or economic grounds" (criteria undefined), and any shorter existing agreed term still applies.

## Summary (100 words)

The Thai credit-term notification is final: published in the Royal Gazette on 2 Oct 2026, effective 2 Nov 2026. Covered buyers have at least THB 500m Thai revenue; suppliers must be SMEs (manufacturing: 200 staff and THB 500m; services/wholesale/retail: 100 staff and THB 300m). Limits: 45 days generally, 30 days for uncomplicated agricultural products, both extendable with reasonable grounds. Penalty: administrative fine up to 10% of revenue (Act s.82); criminal exposure only under s.72. Caveats: gazette body unreadable; clauses taken from a draft-marked copy; fine base and 30-day product list unverified. Sources conflict on billing-date start and criminal penalty.
