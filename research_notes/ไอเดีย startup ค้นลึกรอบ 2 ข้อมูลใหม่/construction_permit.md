# Building permits (การขออนุญาตก่อสร้าง) in Thailand and Southeast Asia, as of 2026-10-20

Scope: permit timelines, digitization status, competitors, and Thai construction market size. Facts are sourced from web pages found via Exa search and fetch. Claims without a primary source are labelled as such.

Labels used:
- **FACT**: stated directly in a cited source (secondary sources are marked as such).
- **ESTIMATE**: derived or projected by a named third party, or calculated from cited figures.
- **OPINION**: vendor, official, or promotional claim; not independently measured.
- **UNVERIFIED**: could not be confirmed, sources conflict, or the primary source was not reached.

## 1. Permit duration: Bangkok and provincial municipalities

### 1a. Legal baseline
- **FACT**: Building Control Act B.E. 2522 s.25 requires the local authority to decide a building-permit application within 45 days. The period may be extended twice, each time by no more than 45 days. Source: BMA handbook (ITA 2568), https://webportal.bangkok.go.th/public/user_files_editor/58/2568/ITA2568/manual%20public/handbook/yotha/y6.pdf. Date: document dated 2568 (2025). Exact publication date not shown.
- **UNVERIFIED (conflict)**: BMA's Oct 2024 news quotes the old process as 45 days plus a 30-day extension. Source: Bangkok Insight, https://www.thebangkokinsight.com/news/politics-general/general/1392651/ (2024-10-04). The handbook says two 45-day extensions. The two BMA-related sources disagree on the extension length.

### 1b. Measured or semi-measured data
- **FACT (World Bank, standardized case)**: Doing Business 2020, Bangkok, standardized warehouse. Time 113 days, 14 procedures, cost 0.6% of warehouse value. Data collected May 2019. Source: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/570131575351213004/pdf/Doing-Business-2020-Comparing-Business-Regulation-in-190-Economies-Economy-Profile-of-Thailand.pdf (DB2020 profile).
- **FACT (World Bank, standardized case)**: Doing Business 2019, Bangkok. Time 118 days, 19 procedures, cost 0.7%. Data collected May 2018. Source (mirror, not the WB original): https://www.agora.mfa.gr/infofiles/DOING%20BUSINESS%20INDICATORS_THAILAND_WB%20th.pdf.
- **Caveat**: Doing Business figures are a standardized hypothetical case, not measured applications. The Doing Business series is no longer published; I did not verify the discontinuation in this session.
- **FACT (secondary, attributed)**: TDRI (2023-07-19) reports the BMA building-permit process typically takes 67 days, against a 45-day legal limit. Its example, Wang Thonglang district, has 6 engineers and inspectors handling an average of 301 applications per year. Source: https://tdri.or.th/en/2023/07/tackling-vague-laws-for-public-safety/. The underlying BMA dataset was not reached, so this is UNVERIFIED at primary level.
- **OPINION (official claim)**: BMA moved online applications for houses of no more than 3 floors and 2,000 m² (trial earlier at 300 m²). The stated target is a 14-day review, down from 45. Source: https://www.thebangkokinsight.com/news/politics-general/general/1392651/ (2024-10-04). The 14-day figure is a target, not a measured result.
- **OPINION (official claim)**: Bangkok governor says all district-office permit processes are online and review time is cut to 14 days. Source: https://www.nationthailand.com/thailand/bangkok/40049946 (2025-05-14). No measured result cited.

### 1c. Provincial municipalities (เทศบาล / อบต.)
- **Gap**: No measured timing dataset found for municipalities or อบต. Only a single municipal self-report, Thap Ma Subdistrict Municipality: online submission for homes of 150 m² or less, "total 3 days". Source: https://thapma.go.th/index/add_file/oBaGAX2Fri103107.pdf (date not shown). This is OPINION and UNVERIFIED. The same page quotes a 3-day legal limit, which conflicts with the 45-day rule in s.25. Likely a transcription error.

### 1d. Summary, duration
- **FACT**: Statutory limit is 45 days (plus extensions). Measured duration in Bangkok is only available from secondary sources: 67 days typical (TDRI 2023), and DB-style standardized cases of 113–118 days, which include utility connections and other steps.

## 2. World Bank B-READY (measured, cross-country)

Method: B-READY uses expert questionnaires for the regulatory and public-services pillars and Enterprise Surveys for operational efficiency. Building-permit benchmark: a 1,800 m² five-floor commercial office in the largest municipality. Source: https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/ac3cfdf5b4bb79da828db8bd5a1f0646-0540022024/related/BReady-Methodology-Workshops-2024-Business-Location.pdf (2024).

Coverage: B-READY 2024 covered 50 economies; B-READY 2025 covers 101; the full 164-economy dataset is due by end-2026. Source: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099122925140531373/pdf/P178471-62c5108c-22a1-4d37-8fc0-2ee5ec06ab9f.pdf (Business Ready 2025 report, mirror dated 2026-01).

- **FACT**: Thailand has B-READY Business Location indicators in the WB data portal (2025). Source: https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/IC.BRE.BL.P1?locations=TH. **Gap**: Thailand's building-permit time and cost values were not retrieved. The guessed economy PDF URL returned not found.
- **FACT (excerpt from economy PDF)**, Malaysia 2025, building permit time 150 days, cost 213% of GNI per capita, construction-related permit 14 days. Source: https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/sites/b-ready/documents/pdf/economy/2025/malaysia-2025.pdf (2025).
- **FACT (excerpt)**, Philippines 2025, building permit 60 days, cost 29% of GNI per capita, construction-related permit 10 days, environmental permit 180 days. Source: https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/sites/b-ready/documents/pdf/economy/2025/philippines-2025.pdf (2025).
- **FACT (excerpt)**, Viet Nam 2025, building permit 154 days, cost 2% of GNI per capita, construction-related permit 15 days. Source: https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/sites/b-ready/documents/pdf/economy/2025/viet-nam-2025.pdf (2025).

## 3. Digital permit-system status, Thailand (as of 2026)

- **FACT**: BMA One-Stop Service (BMA OSS) runs online at https://bmaoss.bangkok.go.th. Citizens can submit 24 hours a day. The system uses 9 shared statuses and covers building-permit forms (แบบ ข.1 and related). Source: BMA OSS manual (2568), https://webportal.bangkok.go.th/public/user_files_editor/97/ITA/2568/O8/13%28300~1.PDF (2025). The 2568 manual says 109 processes; an earlier BMA manual says 114. Both are in the sources.
- **FACT**: BMA OSS was developed by a private vendor, บริษัท บีทามส์ โซลูชั่น จำกัด. Source: BMA OSS manual (2568), same URL as above.
- **FACT**: BMA's e-Permit system (UHA e-Permit) analyses applications; it is linked to the BMA 055 online channel, and applicants can track status and receive alerts. Source: https://www.thebangkokinsight.com/news/politics-general/general/1392651/ (2024-10-04).
- **FACT (secondary)**: DPT's electronic permit-review system, permit.dpt.go.th, was publicized on 2022-11-24. Source: https://www.dhr.go.th/post/publicity/490. Author metadata on that page lists thaidatatech.com; the vendor relationship is UNVERIFIED.
- **Gap**: DPT's permit-review portal could not be checked for 2026 status (fetch timed out). DPT's forms portal with status tracking exists at https://townsquare.dpt.go.th/bmr/ (date not shown).
- **FACT**: The Digital Government Development Agency (DGA) lists a "building-permit system" (ระบบขออนุญาตก่อสร้าง) as a service within its local-government digital platform, for both officials and citizens. Source: https://www.dga.or.th/our-services/digital-platform-services/digital-government-platform/ (page dated 2026-10-19). Adoption by municipalities and อบต. is not published; UNVERIFIED.
- **FACT**: Thailand has no single national e-permit mandate found in this search. Permits are issued by local authorities: districts and Bangkok for BMA, and municipalities or อบต. elsewhere. Source for local-authority role: Building Control Act, as summarized in the BMA handbook (link in 1a).

## 4. Permit volume (official statistics)

Definition: NSO counts "permit recipients" (ผู้ได้รับอนุญาต), meaning persons or entities granted permission, not applications. Data come from 7,859 local permit-issuing bodies (districts, municipalities, tambon administrative organizations) reporting monthly. Source: NSO Q4 2025 report, https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2026/20260305140630_30441.pdf (published 2026-03-05). Permits can be obtained before construction starts, so counts do not equal construction starts.

- **FACT**: 2025 (full year), all new construction and extensions/alterations, all types including non-building: 189,974 permit recipients. Bangkok: 8,972. Central region: 49,710. Source: https://www.thansettakij.com/general-news/657913 (2026-04-30); corroborated by https://www.dailynews.co.th/news/5834385/ (2026-05-04). Secondary press reports of the NSO release; the primary NSO annual table (Thai Economic Indicators 2025, Table 13.6) was not extracted.
- **FACT**: Q4 2025: 48,208 permit recipients, of which 43,647 for buildings (90.5%). Up 1.5% vs Q3 2025 and 0.6% vs Q4 2024. Source: NSO Q4 2025 report (link above).
- **FACT**: Q4 2024: 47,906 total, 43,442 buildings. Q4 2023: 54,123 total. Source: NSO Q4 2024 report, https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2025/20250225102450_38791.pdf (2025-02-25); Q4 2023 from its comparison table.
- **FACT (secondary)**: 2025 building floor area permitted fell 13.2% year on year. Source: Thansettakij (2026-04-30), link above.
- **Gap**: Annual building-only permit count for 2025 was not extracted. Do not annualize Q4 figures; seasonality is material.

## 5. Construction market size, Thailand

Official and bank-research figures:
- **FACT (bank research, citing official data)**: Krungsri Industry Horizon, April 2026. 2025 total construction investment grew 6.5% to THB 1.5 trillion. Public: THB 920 billion (+11.7%). Private: THB 580 billion (−0.8%). Source: https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/industry/industry-horizon/202604 (2026-04-22). 2026 forecast: +1.0–1.5%.
- **FACT (bank research)**: Krungsri Industry Outlook 2025–2027, dated 2025-10-14. 2024 total construction spending was THB 1,407.5 billion, up 2.2%. Source: https://www.krungsri.com/en/research/industry/industry-outlook/construction-construction-materials/construction-contractors/io/construction-contractor-2025-2027.
  - **UNVERIFIED (internal inconsistency)**: the same report gives 1H25 growth as both 12.3% and 24.5%, and a 1H25 level of THB 436.8 billion. Scope is unclear, possibly public only.
- **FACT (bank research)**: SCB EIC, 2025-03-10. Total construction value is about THB 1.4 trillion per year, 57% public and 43% private. Forecast for 2025: public THB 856 billion (+3%), private THB 586 billion (+1%). Top 19 SET-listed contractors hold about 15% of the market; the rest is fragmented among mid-size and small contractors. Source: https://www.scbeic.com/th/detail/file/product/9733/h5c61yeod0/Artwork-Business-intelligence-CONSTRUCTION-INDUSTRY-20250310.pdf.
- **FACT (official, context)**: Thailand GDP 2023, current prices, THB 17,954.7 billion. Source: NSO Statistical Yearbook 2025, https://www.nso.go.th/public/e-book/Statistical-Yearbook/SYB-2025/16/ (2025).
- **FACT (official statistic, context)**: NSO reports 48,000–54,000 quarterly building permit recipients in recent years (section 4).
- **ESTIMATE (calculated)**: 2024 construction spending (THB 1,407.5 billion) is about 7.8% of 2023 GDP (THB 17,954.7 billion). The years differ, so treat as approximate.

Third-party research estimates (not official):
- **ESTIMATE (research firm)**: Mordor Intelligence puts Thailand's construction market at USD 29.91 billion (2025) and USD 31.55 billion (2026). Source: https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/thailand-construction-market (2020 page, updated).
- **ESTIMATE (research firm)**: Research and Markets reports a construction market of THB 466.0 billion (2025) and THB 489.6 billion (2026). Source: https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5918186/thailand-construction-industry-databook-market.
  - **UNVERIFIED (conflict)**: this figure is roughly one-third of the bank-research figures above. The scope is likely different (building-only, or a narrower definition). Do not use interchangeably.
- **UNVERIFIED**: a T3 article states construction is "~8% of GDP (2023)" and notes it could not verify the figure. Source: https://www.thetruethailand.com/business/construction-thailand.

Standard maximum 28 days for PBG; ministry says 3 days for a simple single house (OPINION, target). Source: https://pu.go.id/berita/dukung-kemudahan-perizinan-usaha-kementerian-pupr-luncurkan-layanan-simbg (2021-07-30). Adoption rate not found.
