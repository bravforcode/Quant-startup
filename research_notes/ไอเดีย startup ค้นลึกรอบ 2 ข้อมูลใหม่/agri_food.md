# Thai agri-food and food-SME supply chain pain, as of 2026

Research date: 2026-10-20. Scope: farm households and food SMEs in Thailand; middleman margins; buyer payment terms; input costs; financing; traceability; existing agri-fintech and farm-management platforms; 2026 government digital programs for farmers.

Method: Exa web search (14 queries) and Exa page fetch on 6 primary pages (NSO census, Nation/BOT, PIER blog, Lanta/Khaosod, Allianz Trade, Thansettakij). Most other sources were read from search snippets, not full pages. No business ideas are recommended or ranked here.

## Label key

- FACT: stated in an official, primary or named secondary source (source named).
- ESTIMATE: model, projection or analyst estimate.
- OPINION: quote, anecdote, op-ed, or company self-claim.
- UNVERIFIED: date, number, or attribution could not be confirmed, or sources conflict.

## 2. Middlemen, farmgate share, and price capture

Measured shares:

- **Pomelo, Pak Phanang (Nakhon Si Thammarat).** Marketing margin 65.38 baht per fruit, 36.65% of retail; farmers keep 65.35% of retail. Sample n=137. [FACT; data year not stated] Source: https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/wms/article/view/135642 (2018-07-20).
- **Organic vegetables (Mahidol thesis).** Grower receives 59% of retail price; total marketing cost 41%; wholesaler margin 5.1%; retailer 20%. Thesis is dated BE 2551 (about 2008). [FACT, small sample; exact date UNVERIFIED] Source: http://mulinet11.li.mahidol.ac.th/e-thesis/2551/cd427/4936880.pdf.
- **Fresh produce at modern retail (GRAIN, citing BioThai).** About 14% of the retail price reaches farmers on average; banana 25%. [FACT as reported by BioThai; page date UNVERIFIED] Source: https://grain.org/en/article/6029.
- **Rice marketing channels, 2008 (ADB).** Paddy sold 45% to local merchants, 33% to millers, 22% under government pledging. Middleman service charge about 0.75% of transaction value. Wholesalers typically pay in 1–3 months. [FACT, 2008 data; report date not shown] Source: https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/project-documents/43430-012-reg-tacr-03.pdf.
- **Rice: competition and price.** Farmers in areas where cooperatives compete directly with private intermediaries receive 10.9% higher prices from private intermediaries (2018/19 data). [FACT] Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306919221000294 (2021-05-01).
- **Rice: price pass-through (2025 dissertation).** Pass-through to farmgate prices is incomplete. In concentrated markets, intermediaries absorb a larger share of shocks. [FACT, qualitative] Source: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/3329f478 (2025-01-01).
- **Sweet pepper, Chiang Mai (2007 survey, n=244).** More than half of farmers sell without a contract. Village traders have been the main channel since 2005. Green-pepper price differences across channels were not statistically significant. [FACT, 2007 data] Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodpol.2011.07.004 (2011-08-04).
- **Smallholder information (ICBTS 2025, n=200).** 34% receive market price information "sometimes" and 29% "often". 34% have moderate trust in wholesaler pricing and 9% high trust. Wholesalers are the main price source (35%). [FACT per paper; sampling method UNVERIFIED] Source: http://icbtsproceeding.ssru.ac.th/index.php/ICBTSBEIJING2025/article/download/1511/1482 (2025).
- **Coconut middlemen (2019 model).** Middlemen hold monopsony/duopsony power; growers gain from price competition. [FACT, qualitative] Source: https://so06.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/utccjournalhs/article/view/190892 (2019-05-24).
- **Northeast fruit and vegetables (2004).** Large gap between farmgate and wholesale prices; no percentage given. [FACT, qualitative, old] Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/amsj/13/2/13_KJ00009596699/_article/-char/en (2004-12-31).
- **Potato (1987).** Growers received 15–35% of retail price in the 1980s. Historical only; excluded from the analysis. [FACT, historical] Source: https://hdl.handle.net/10568/111659 (1987-11-01).

Gap: no 2024–2026 measured farm-to-retail share for Thai produce, rice, or livestock was found. [UNVERIFIED gap]

## 3. Buyer payment terms and delayed payments

- **Regulated SME payment terms.** The Trade Competition Commission's guidelines (first issued 2021, amended 2022) set 30 days for agricultural products and primary agro-processing, and 45 days for trade, manufacturing and services. Unfair practices include unjustified delay beyond terms and changing terms with less than 60 days' notice. The public survey closed 2025-07-20. [FACT] Source: https://www.tilleke.com/insights/thailand-invites-stakeholder-feedback-on-credit-term-guidelines/ (2025-07-04). Outcome of the survey: UNVERIFIED.
- **Typical payment behaviour.** Most payments are made within 45–60 days. SMEs may have shorter terms. Delays of about 10–20 days beyond the agreed period occur when transactions are unsecured or buyers are short of liquidity. Contractual late-payment interest can be up to 15%; the statutory rate is 7.5%. [ESTIMATE for the delay range; FACT for the rates; credit-insurer characterisation] Source: https://www.allianz-trade.com/en_global/economic-research/collection-complexity/thailand.html (2026-01-23).
- **SEA SME survey (about 1,000 SMEs in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam).** For Thai SMEs, credit-term management was the top receivables issue. Chasing late payments was the most widespread issue region-wide. [FACT, company survey; country sample sizes not given] Source: https://blog.fundingsocieties.com.my/managing-receivables-smes-turn-to-new-solutions/ (2023-11-16).
- **Cited regional delay figure.** Average APAC payment delay rose from 54 days (2021) to 67 days (2022), against 30-day conventional terms. [FACT as reported; primary study not identified, so UNVERIFIED] Source: same URL as above.
- **Credit tightening in 2025.** Some firms cut terms to 7–15 days from 30–60 days. Food-industry leaders said new customers are cash-only; long-standing customers keep 30–60 days. Restaurant suppliers have sometimes extended terms from 30 to 60 days. [OPINION, anecdotal business-association quotes] Source: https://www.thansettakij.com/business/631831 (2025-07-03).
- **Rice exporters.** Credit terms of 45–90 days are used in place of letters of credit. [OPINION, quoted by exporters association] Source: same URL as above.
- **Listed agri and food firms (2020–2023; 46 companies, 184 observations).** In food and beverage, average collection period has a negative effect on ROA and ROE. In agribusiness, days payable has a positive effect on ROA. [FACT; listed firms, not SMEs] Source: https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/rpu/article/view/292971 (2025-08-29).

Gap: no measured Thai receivable-days data specific to food SMEs was found. [UNVERIFIED gap]

## 4. Input-cost volatility, 2026

Diesel:
- Retail diesel rose from 29.94 baht/l (17 March 2026) to 50.54 baht/l (6 April 2026), +68.8% in under a month. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.thansettakij.com/economy/energy/656009 (2026-04-07).
- Diesel raised production costs of rice, beans, sugar and coffee by an estimated 6.8–11.7%. Diesel is 10–20% of total cost for some crops. [ESTIMATE, analyst] Source: same URL.

Fertilizer, Thai retail:
- Urea 46-0-0: from about 800 to 1,200–1,400 baht per 50 kg bag, roughly +100%. [OPINION, quoted by an agri-supply company executive] Source: https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40068083 (2026-06-30).
- Chainat farmers: fertilizer 650–700 to 1,150–1,200 baht per sack; paddy below 6,300 baht/t; over 1,000 rai left fallow. [OPINION, farmer quotes; press observation] Source: https://www.khaosod.co.th/special-stories/news_10221526 (2026-04-24).
- Chachoengsao: urea 800–900 to over 1,000 baht per sack, possibly 1,200. [OPINION, trader quote] Source: https://www.bbc.com/thai/articles/cevk190vkvlo (2026-04-07).
- Average Thai fertilizer price rose 7.3%, from 1,125 to 1,208 baht per sack. [ESTIMATE, analyst series; conflicts with the ~100% urea figure above, so UNVERIFIED which series applies] Source: https://www.thansettakij.com/economy/energy/656009 (2026-04-07).
- Fertilizer is about 40% of operating costs for farms, per industry. Retail shelf prices lag global declines by 1–2 months because retailers clear older, more expensive stock. [OPINION, industry] Source: https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40068083 (2026-06-30).
- Fertilizer is 13% of total rice cost. Modelled effect: yield down 21% and farm income down 19% in the main planting season. [ESTIMATE, KResearch model] Source: https://www.tnnthailand.com/wealth/economy/231710/ (2026-04-16).

Global and supply side:
- World Bank reference urea price was 857 USD/mt in April 2026, a four-year high (726 in March). [FACT, as reported citing World Bank] Source: https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/world/economics/1232644 (2026-05-06).
- World Bank projection: fertilizer prices up 31% in 2026, urea up about 60%. [ESTIMATE, projection] Same source.
- Thailand imports all nitrogen, phosphate and potassium fertilizers. [FACT, as stated by fertilizer association committee member] Source: https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40068083 (2026-06-30). This conflicts with a KResearch-cited ">50% of chemical fertilizer imports" figure in TNN (2026-04-16). [UNVERIFIED conflict]
- Ministry stock: about 1.52 million t of chemical fertilizer in early March 2026, about 5 months of use at about 0.8 Mt/month. [FACT, as stated by the Ministry of Commerce] Source: https://www.bbc.com/thai/articles/c74725q7xd8o (2026-03-17).

Rice and farmgate prices:
- Purchase price quoted at 7,200–7,600 baht/t, unchanged. [OPINION, farmer quote] Source: https://www.bbc.com/thai/articles/c74725q7xd8o (2026-03-17).
- Farmers quoted in April 2026 as planning to skip or delay the next crop because of fuel and fertilizer costs. [OPINION, interviews] Source: https://www.bbc.com/thai/articles/cevk190vkvlo (2026-04-07).

## 5. Financing

Farm debt:
- BAAC NPL at 31 March 2026: 117,429 million baht, 6.83% of loans, up from 5.33%. Cause given: weaker repayment capacity from economic conditions and natural disasters. [FACT] Source: https://www.baac.or.th/web-api/assets/upload/file/document/annual-report/baac-annual-report-2025.pdf (BAAC annual report FY2025; published 2026).
- Agriculture NPL ratio: 11.81% of agricultural business loans in Q2/2025, the highest of nine sectors. [FACT, as cited by BAAC from BOT data] Same source.
- Debt-to-income of indebted agricultural households rose from 9.1x (2015) to 11.8x (2023). [FACT, per NESDC table as cited in BAAC report] Same source.
- Average debt per indebted household: NESDC table shows about 314 thousand baht (2023); the BAAC text says 245,801 baht in 2024 (243,373 in 2023). Definitions differ. [UNVERIFIED conflict] Same source.
- PIER/BAAC 2026 study (3.97M farmer borrowers, ten years): median debt rose from 200,000 to 250,000 baht; 42% of borrowers' residual income is insufficient to repay; interest-only payers rose from 20% to over 50% in eight years; only 10% pay down principal steadily. Each branch repayment trip costs 300–1,000 baht. [FACT, research summary] Source: https://www.pier.or.th/blog/2026/t092/ (2026-05-05).
- Bangkok Post summary of PIER: more than half of farmer debtors are unlikely to repay in full within their lifetimes; most are elderly. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/3250307/more-than-half-of-farmers-loans-unlikely-to-be-repaid (2026-05-06).
- PIER working paper (2019–2022 data): more than 90% of surveyed farm households have debt, averaging about 450,000 baht. [FACT, undated working paper] Source: https://www.pier.or.th/files/dp/pier_dp_204.pdf.

SME credit:
- BOT governor (2026-09-09): SME loans have shrunk for 16 consecutive quarters. Rejection rates are 60–70%: 78% for new firms with no history, 86% for firms with bad-debt records. Even the top 30% of SMEs get credit at 21%, against 61% for large firms. SME average interest is about 6.9%, against about 3% for large firms. Among about 130,000 SME legal entities, 50% had closed within 10 years. [FACT, as stated by BOT] Sources: https://www.nationthailand.com/business/banking-finance/40070829 (2026-09-09); https://en.moneyandbanking.co.th/2026/268813/ (2026-09-09).
- OECD: SME commercial-bank loans were THB 3.08 trillion at end-2024 (-3.11% YoY), 30.91% of business loans. SME NPL ratio 7.17%. [FACT] Source: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/financing-smes-and-entrepreneurs-2026_075d8058-en/full-report/thailand_a1b1b4e6.html (2026-03-31).
- OSMEP survey of 2,758 SMEs, Q4/2025: 31.8% borrow only from formal lenders; 28.0% borrow only informally (up from 21.9%); 40.2% use both (down from 46.9%). Main rejection reason: insufficient or irregular cash flow, 56.5%. Documents that do not reflect the business, 27.8%. Micro-SMEs' formal access fell to 14.5% from 21.8%. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/economics/1221880 (2026-02-19).
- Asian Banking & Finance (citing BOT): only 30% of SMEs have formal credit; 40% use both formal and informal; 20–23% rely on informal lenders. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://asianbankingandfinance.net/lending-credit/news/thai-sme-loans-shrink-16-quarters-credit-access-gap-widens (2026-09-25).
- OSMEP MSME poll, Q4/2024: agriculture and trade MSMEs have the least access to in-system finance. Borrowing costs 9–12%. [FACT, survey] Source: https://smeone.info/storage/app/public/uploads/files/2025/250213_PPT_Poll_SME_Q4_2567.pdf (2025-02-13).

## 6. Traceability

Official programs:
- MOAC/ACFS "QR Trace on Cloud": lot-level traceability for smallholders, cooperatives, community enterprises and SMEs; covers vegetables, fruit, rice, livestock, eggs, fish and processed goods. [FACT, program description] Source: https://www.kehakaset.com/newsactivities_details.php?view_item=658 (2023-06-20).
- Participation: more than 4,000 operators (ACFS, October 2025). [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.agrinewsthai.com/news/227490 (2025-10-16).
- ACFS official said most users are suppliers to department stores able to enter data themselves. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.tpso.go.th/news/2409-0000000007 (2024-09-05).
- Fisheries Department pilot of QR Trace on Cloud for an organic tilapia community group; plans to extend to seabass, prawn and frog groups in FY2569. [FACT, pilot and plan] Source: https://www4.fisheries.go.th/local/index.php/main/view_activities/1270/245934 (2025-04-01).
- TraceThai.com (Commerce Ministry with Thammasat; blockchain prototype): 160 users or groups, mainly organic; not yet at national scale. [FACT] Source: https://www.tpso.go.th/news/2409-0000000007 (2024-09-05).
- EUDR Thailand Traceability Platform (ARDA), live 21 Jan 2026: covers rubber, palm oil, cattle, timber, coffee, cocoa, soy. Exports of these to the EU were over USD 1.85 billion in 2024 (UN Comtrade, as cited). Plot coordinates and land-rights documents are needed; SMEs and smallholders are the hardest hit. [FACT, as reported] Source: https://en.thairath.co.th/agriculture/agricultural-policy/2909150 (2026-01-21).
- Rubber authority (GYT): EUDR-compliant rubber may earn a price premium. [OPINION] Source: https://www.tpso.go.th/news/2409-0000000007 (2024-09-05).

Prototypes and pilots:
- KasetTrace.com (academic prototype): 300 user rating 4.06/5; average trace response 42.12 seconds; five products. [FACT, prototype test] Source: https://li01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/PRRJ_Scitech/article/view/267673 (2025-12-30).
- Durian Trace (NSTDA): tested on 31 containers exported to China. Scaling needs government policy support. [FACT, pilot] Source: https://www.nstda.or.th/home/news_post/sci-news-flash-durian-trace/ (2025-09-25).

## 8. Government digital programs for farmers, 2026

Farmer registry and subsidies:
- **Registration call for crop year 2569/70** (DOAE, 2026-05-25). Register or update after planting: rice, maize and factory cassava within 15–60 days; field crops within 15–30 days; fruit trees any time. Channels: Farmbook app (existing farmers, same plots only), e-Form at efarmer.doae.go.th, district offices, and village volunteers (อกม.). Status ends after 3 years with no update. [FACT] Source: https://doaenews.doae.go.th/archives/32133 (2026-05-25).
- **Registration procedure.** DOAE manual: 40 working days in total. Land verification takes 7 days (titled land linked to the Lands Department), 15 days (other titled land) or 28 days (no documents). Field checks by GPS or photo, with village leaders. [FACT] Source: https://farmer.doae.go.th/uploads/file20250701155912.pdf (last updated 2025-06-05).
- **2025/26 rice catch-up payment.** 233,729 households not yet paid, 1,000 baht per rai, capped at 10 rai (10,000 baht per household), paid through BAAC. Eligibility check at efarmer.doae.go.th/checkFarmer. [FACT] Source: https://en.thairath.co.th/news/society/2939845 (2026-06-16).
- **2025/26 payment window and Farmbook outage.** Cabinet approval 19 Aug 2025. On 3 Sept 2025, DOAE said the Farmbook app was closed for system improvements, so payment status could not be checked. [FACT] Source: https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/news/news-update/1197183 (2025-09-03).
- **2026/27 proposals.** 1,000 baht per rai for about 4.69 million registered households (up to 10 rai each), plus a 50% price-gap compensation scheme for five rice groups. Agencies (Dept of Rice, DOAE, BAAC, Dept of Internal Trade) are drafting for the National Rice Policy Committee. [FACT for proposals; not yet approved] Source: https://lanta.news/articles/thailand-to-give-farmers-1-000-baht-per-rai-subsidy (2026-10-09).
- **Green-Flag Fertilizer Plus (ปุ๋ยธงเขียวพลัส), 2026.** Support raised to 300 baht per sack, five sacks, plus 50 baht towards agri-chemicals: 1,550 baht per household. Holders of the green farmer-registration book (เล่มเขียว) and soil card get six sacks plus a 250 baht organic coupon: up to 2,100 baht. Fertilizer producers cut 50 baht per sack at the factory gate (target 10 million sacks). [FACT, as reported] Source: https://www.tnnthailand.com/wealth/economy/231710/ (2026-04-16).
- **Fertilizer price control.** Fertilizer is on the 59-item controlled price list (2025 notice). Price rises need Department of Internal Trade approval. [FACT] Source: https://www.bbc.com/thai/articles/c74725q7xd8o (2026-03-17).

## 9. Unverified items and conflicts

- Total food-manufacturing SME count not found (only the ready-to-eat subset).
- Tracxn funding figures are third-party estimates. Company revenue and yield figures (Ricult, BKS, Living Roots, Techcoop) are self-reported.

- Several sources lack a publication date: GRAIN, ADB rice report, PIER working paper DP 204, MOAC app page, SME Big Data dashboard.
