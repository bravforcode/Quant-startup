# Residential energy in Thailand and Southeast Asia (rooftop solar, home batteries, EV chargers, electricity bills), as of 2026

Research date: 2026-10-20. Scope: Thailand primarily, with SEA comparators.

Labels used:
- **FACT**: stated by an official or primary source (government, regulator, utility, or a named report) as reported in the cited page.
- **ESTIMATE**: a number derived or forecast by a named analyst, or a number I calculated from cited inputs.
- **OPINION**: a stated view, recommendation, or interpretation.
- **UNVERIFIED**: company self-report, press release, anecdote, secondary aggregator, or a claim I could not cross-check.

Dates are publication dates from the cited page. "undated" means the page showed no date. Thai fiscal years (ปีงบประมาณ) end 30 Sep. Thai years are Buddhist Era (พ.ศ. = CE + 543; 2569 = 2026).

## 3. Consumer pain points (with evidence)

### 3a. Installation trust, quality, and safety
- [FACT] **OCPB complaint count**: Since 2024 up to May 2026, OCPB received 156 complaints about residential solar installation service. 84 closed. Claimed losses above THB 40 million. Most common complaint: substandard equipment; second: unfair service. Quoted by Minister Supamas Isarabhakdi. Source: Government Public Relations Dept (PRD) radio page, 2026-05-20: https://radiochumphae.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/39/iid/504915
- [FACT] **OCPB investigation**: 17 homeowners (Khon Kaen, Saraburi, Tha Phra/Thonburi) assisted; preliminary findings alleged aluminium wiring instead of copper, three-phase converted to single-phase, poor grounding, inverters too close to heat sources; bills did not fall; installers refused responsibility. Source: pv magazine, 2026-05-19: https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/19/thailand-opens-investigation-into-faulty-solar-installations/ ; Thai PBS World, 2026-05-16: https://www.thaipbsworld.com/news/solar-panel-probe-%28drafted%29
- [FACT] OCPB announced intent to (a) classify solar panels, inverters and batteries as "controlled-label" products and (b) classify installation as a "contract-controlled business". Current status UNVERIFIED after May 2026. Source: Thailand Construction News, 2026-05-16: https://thailand-construction.com/thai-government-orders-office-of-the-consumer-protection-board-to-urgently-investigate-unsafe-solar-rooftop-installations-linked-to-fire-risks-and-consumer-losses-exceeding-500000-baht/
- [UNVERIFIED] Individual complaints reported in Thai media (May 2026): THB 410,000 (Bangkok clinic, "did not reduce bills", equipment failing); THB 320,000 (Saraburi, wiring out of phase, near fire); THB 230,000 (equipment non-standard, short circuit). Installers allegedly followed YouTube tutorials. Sources: TNews, 2026-05-13: https://www.tnews.co.th/social/social-news/650333 ; Thai PBS programme page, 2026-05-19: https://www.thaipbs.or.th/program/People/watch/277551
- [OPINION] Consumer Council of Thailand (สภาผู้บริโภค), 2026-05-15, asked for: mandatory standards beyond voluntary TIS; a registered installer list published for consumers; MEA/PEA as the central pre-connection inspector; one-stop complaints centre. Source: https://www.tcc.or.th/150569_solar-on-fire_news/
- [UNVERIFIED] Pantip thread listing bad installers (no date, anecdotal, crowdsourced). Source: https://pantip.com/topic/42522348

### 3b. Consumer intent vs. action (survey)
- [FACT] SCB EIC survey (n=2,257, early 2025): 80% interested but not installed; 9% installed; 3% installing; 8% not interested. Top barriers: (1) checking installer reliability and fair price (lack of clear comparable information); (2) choosing technology (panels, inverter); (3) permitting complexity (multiple agencies, documents, site inspection); (4) financing (SCB says over 50% of installers take payment in cash / own funds). Source: SCB EIC, 2025-08-22: https://www.scbeic.com/th/detail/product/Solar-rooftop-220825
- [UNVERIFIED] Sample method and representativeness were not described in the excerpt reviewed.

### 3c. Collective-purchase pilot (civil society)
- [FACT] Thailand Solar Fund "Guan Hiw Saeng" one-stop collective purchase pilot (Mar 14 – Jul 31, 2025): 128 households applied; 52 paid deposits (40.6% conversion). Project's conclusion: model hard to sustain without funding because of price undercutting and quality concerns; recommends a contractor rating platform and a hybrid advisory/QA model. Source: Thai Consumer Council-hosted final report, 2026-02: https://www.tcc.or.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/study-fuel-fund-Eng.pdf

### 3d. Economics and low buyback
- [ESTIMATE] IEEFA: 5 kW system at about THB 150,000 has a payback just over 7 years under net billing at 2.20 THB/kWh; a 10 kW system at lower cost per kW has about 5.9 years; TOU-based 5 kW about 6.1 years. Source: IEEFA, 2026-08 (above).
- [FACT] IEEFA: Thai rooftop costs about USD 936/kW, about 50% higher than Pakistan, Malaysia and Vietnam (cited as regional comparison; dealer markups and limited service cited as causes). Source: IEEFA, 2026-08 (above).
- [FACT] Average retail tariff about 3.88–3.95 THB/kWh (ERC, see section 5), vs. 2.20 buyback. The gap makes self-consumption the main value driver (OPINION of IEEFA; numbers FACT).
- [UNVERIFIED] Matichon (2025-12-02): 5 kW cost THB 125,000–150,000 (about 25–30 THB/W in 2025); LCOE 2–3 THB/kWh; payback 6–8 years; 25+ yr life. Secondary news; no primary source cited.
- [FACT] Rule-change risk (history): residential quota moved 100 MW → 50 MW (+50 MW shared) → 90 MW (2023) → 500 MW (2026-04) → 10,000 MW (2026-09). Buyback moved 1.68 → 2.20 THB/kWh. Export cap 5 kW/meter now applies to a system that may be above 5 kW. Source: IEEFA, 2026-08 (above); Thai PBS, 2026-09-18 (above).
- [OPINION] IEEFA argues a net-metering (kWh-for-kWh) scheme and higher buyback would raise returns; not adopted per the Sep 2026 decision.

### 3e. Permits and grid
- [FACT] Thai Policy Watch (2026-04-30): permitting for solar rooftop shortened to 30 days if surplus is sold, 7 days if not; utilities asked to consider reducing connection fees. Source: https://policywatch.thaipbs.or.th/article/economy-245
- [FACT] Government (2026-09-18): MEA/PEA/EGAT and MoE to produce grid-readiness plan within 1 month covering forecasting, real-time monitoring, distribution control, battery storage. Source: PRD, 2026-09-18 (above).
- [ESTIMATE] Grid-capacity concern: government says the 10 GW expansion must be paired with grid preparation; no grid-hosting study was found.

### 3f. Frequency summary
- Quantified: 156 OCPB complaints over ~2 years (2024 to May 2026) [FACT]. Survey: 8% of respondents not interested; 80% interested but not installed [FACT, SCB].
- No public count of total rooftop installs that ended in disputes was found. Frequency across the market is UNVERIFIED.

## 6. Discrepancies and open questions

1. **Rooftop capacity** differs by source and scope:
   - DEDE/ministry FY2568: 3,932 MW rooftop IPS (incl. C&I), Thansettakij 2026-04-22.
   - ERC: >5,580 MW total BTM-PV (about 87% rooftop) at Sep 2025, Matichon 2025-12-02.
   - IEEFA: rooftop 3.6 GW (early 2026) and total solar 11.8 GW (Mar 2026).
   - TransitionZero satellite: 3.60 GW (range 3.02–4.17), Nov 2025.
   - IRENA total solar: 6,842 MW end-2025 (pv magazine 2026-09-10). This conflicts with IEEFA's 11.8 GW total. Unresolved.
2. **10 GW target**: pv magazine calls it a draft PDP 2026 target (2026-09-10). Thai government states NEPC approved expansion (2026-09-18). Both reports fit; the implementation status is unresolved.
3. **Household count**: government says about 1 million households for 10 GW (2026-09-18). Earlier phase-one target was 500,000 households (pv magazine, 2026-08-05). Both as reported.
4. **PPA term**: 10 yrs (April and June 2026) vs 20 yrs (Sept 2026 decision). The Sept decision applies also to earlier 500 MW participants (Thai PBS 2026-09-18).
5. **SHARGE funding**: unfunded (Tracxn) vs USD 1.4M (LinkedIn company profile). Unresolved.
6. **ION Energy funding**: amount from Innovation ONE undisclosed; LinkedIn shows "$3M total". Unresolved.
7. **Consumer complaints** (OCPB, 156) cover solar installation services; not all are rooftop residential. Scope unclear.

## 7. Gaps and limits of this research
- No official household-only installed count (MW or homes) found.
- No primary dataset for OCPB complaints (only press-release figures).
- Competitor funding comes from PRs, LinkedIn or Tracxn; not checked against DBD (Thai company registry) or SEC/mai filings.
- Frost & Sullivan and 6Wresearch reports (paywalled) were listed but no figures could be extracted. Not used.
- ERC implementing rules (notification text) for the 500 MW and 10 GW programs were not found; only press summaries.
- Thai-language sources were used for most official figures; English translations of quotes are my own paraphrase.
- Malaysia and Philippines data are from pv magazine, Ember and a compliance site; no national regulator text was read.
- Consumer surveys (SCB EIC, 2025) do not state sample method in the excerpt reviewed.
